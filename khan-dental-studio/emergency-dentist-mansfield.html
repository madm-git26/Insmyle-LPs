Until you get here

In dental pain? Here is what to do in the next five minutes.

Simple, standard first-aid steps that protect the tooth and take the edge off while you travel. None of them replace being seen: call us first.

  • 1
    Call (817) 839-7412 first.Tell us what happened and when. On a clinic day, calling before 2 pm gives us the best chance of seeing you today.
  • 2
    Rinse gently with warm salt water.It clears debris and calms irritated gums. Do not rinse a socket hard, and never put aspirin directly on the gum: it burns the tissue.
  • 3
    Knocked-out tooth: keep it moist.Hold it by the crown, not the root. Rinse briefly if dirty, then keep it in milk or tucked in your cheek. Every minute counts.
  • 4
    Cold compress for swelling.Twenty minutes on, twenty off, on the outside of the cheek. Swelling that spreads toward the eye or neck is urgent.
  • 5
    Save the pieces.A broken crown, chip or lost filling: bring it with you. Some can be re-cemented on the spot.
  • 6
    Over-the-counter pain relief as directed.Take it as the packet instructs and tell us what you took and when, because it changes how we treat you.
When to go to the ER instead: swelling that makes it hard to breathe or swallow, bleeding that will not stop, a suspected broken jaw, or a head injury. Get emergency medical help first, then call us for the tooth.

Questions

Emergency dentistry: what people ask.

Can I be seen today?
Call before 2 pm on a clinic day (Friday, Saturday or Sunday) and we will do everything we can to see you the same day. We keep protected slots open every morning specifically for emergencies. If something is urgent we will usually treat it the same day; otherwise you leave with a written plan and a price. Call (817) 839-7412.
What if my emergency happens on a Monday to Thursday?
The office is open Friday 12–7 pm and Saturday and Sunday 10 am–3 pm. Outside those hours, call and leave a message: Dr. Khan provides after-hours phone triage and will tell you how to manage until the next clinic day. If you have swelling that affects breathing or swallowing, uncontrolled bleeding or a suspected broken jaw, go to the emergency room first.
Do I need to be an existing patient?
No. Roughly a third of our new patients have not seen a dentist in five years or more, and there is no lecture waiting for you. We deal with anything urgent first, then build a phased plan you actually feel comfortable saying yes to.
How many visits does emergency dentistry usually take?
It depends on the starting point, but most emergency dentistry cases at Khan Dental Studio are completed in one to three appointments. You will be given the exact number, and the exact cost, in writing before anything is booked.
Will it hurt?
Modern anaesthesia, topical gel before every injection and unhurried appointments mean the overwhelming majority of our patients describe treatment as far easier than they expected. If you are anxious, tell us when you book and we schedule extra time for it.
Does insurance cover emergency dentistry?
Coverage varies by plan and by procedure code. We verify your benefits before treatment is scheduled and give you a written estimate showing what your carrier pays and what you pay. Members of our in-house plan receive 15–20% off emergency dentistry. No insurance? CareCredit and Sunbit offer 6 to 24-month plans, several interest-free, with approval in minutes.
Can you fix a broken tooth in one visit?
Very often, yes. We scan the prepared tooth digitally, design the crown on screen, mill it from a solid ceramic block in our lab room and cement it the same visit, usually around two hours from start to finish. No temporary crown and no second trip.
Do you see children with dental emergencies?
Yes, from the first tooth onwards. Dr. Hannah Cole, our associate dentist, has a restorative and paediatric focus, and first visits for little ones are deliberately short and low-key.
How long will the result last?
With good home care and regular hygiene visits, well-executed dental work lasts years to decades. We photograph and monitor every restoration at your check-ups so small issues are caught long before they become expensive ones.
What should I bring?
Your insurance card or plan details, a list of medications including supplements and blood thinners, and any recent X-rays from another office if you have them. If you have a lost crown or a broken piece of tooth, bring that too.
