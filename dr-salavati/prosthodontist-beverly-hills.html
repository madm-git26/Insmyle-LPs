What a prosthodontist actually is

Most people searching for one already suspect the answer: a dentist who did the general degree, then three more years of residency in one thing — rebuilding and replacing teeth. It is one of the smaller dental specialties, and the one other dentists send their most complex cases to.

Board-certified, not self-described

Dr. Salavati completed a three-year Advanced Implant and Prosthodontics residency at the University of Illinois Chicago and holds board certification in the specialty.

Restoration is the whole job

Veneers, crowns, bridges, implants, dentures and full-mouth cases are not a menu item here. They are the entire practice.

One patient, one focus

She limits her day to a small number of appointments and treats you herself, start to finish — the reason the work takes the time it needs.

No pressure, no surprises

What a consultation actually involves

Prosthodontic work is planned before it is done. The first visit is diagnosis and a plan, not treatment — you leave knowing what is recommended, in what order, and why.

  1. 01

    A conversation about what you want

    What bothers you, what you have tried, what "done" looks like to you. This shapes everything after it.

  2. 02

    A full examination and imaging

    Teeth, gums, bite and jaw, with digital imaging and cone-beam CT where the case calls for it — implants are planned in three dimensions.

  3. 03

    A preview you can react to

    For smile-design cases, a digital preview of the proposed result — before any tooth is touched.

  4. 04

    A plan, in phases, with the cost

    What is recommended, what can wait, the sequence, and the number — before you commit to anything.

Who you'll be seeing

Board-certified prosthodontist. DMD from the University of Louisville School of Dentistry, then a three-year Advanced Implant and Prosthodontics residency at the University of Illinois Chicago. What goes in your mouth

Metal-free where possible, and planned in 3D

The practice takes a biological approach: biocompatible, metal-free materials wherever the case allows, and the imaging to plan restorative work precisely rather than approximately.

Ceramic and zirconia restorations

Crowns and bridges in metal-free ceramic and zirconia, matched for shade and translucency.

SDS Swiss ceramic implants

A metal-free implant option for patients who want to avoid titanium.

Cone-beam CT and digital imaging

Three-dimensional imaging for implant planning and complex cases.

Waterlase laser, PRF and ozone

Laser dentistry for gentler treatment, and PRF and ozone therapies to support healing.

Anxious about dental work? Sedation is available, from nitrous oxide through to IV sedation.
